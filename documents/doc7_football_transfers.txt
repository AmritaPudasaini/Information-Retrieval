Title: Post World Cup Transfer Window Becomes the Most Expensive in Football History
Source (for reference, not reproduced): football365.com, foot-africa.com, thegame.ph

The 2026 summer football transfer window turned into one of the busiest and most expensive in the sport's history, driven largely by the market effects of the FIFA World Cup held earlier that year in North America. Players who performed well on the international stage saw their valuations rise sharply within weeks of the tournament ending, a pattern analysts describe as a recurring World Cup premium. Combined spending across Europe's top five leagues pushed past several billion euros before the window closed at the end of August, with Premier League clubs responsible for a large share of the biggest deals.

Several high profile sagas dominated the summer. A standout German playmaker was pursued heavily by Spanish and English giants after a breakout tournament performance, while a young Spanish winger's release clause became the subject of an active bidding war between two major clubs. Real Madrid conducted one of the busiest rebuilding efforts of any club, bringing in several established internationals on both permanent transfers and free moves after their previous contracts expired. Elsewhere, a English forward's move to Barcelona and a club record fee paid for a winger by an English side were among the headline permanent transfers. Clubs increasingly relied on loan to buy structures to manage the financial impact of inflated fees while still meeting financial fair play requirements set by European football's governing bodies.
