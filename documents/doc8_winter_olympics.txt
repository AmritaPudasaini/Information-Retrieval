Title: Milan Cortina Hosts the 2026 Winter Olympics Across Northern Italy
Source (for reference, not reproduce): aljazeera.com, foxnews.com, nbcsportsboston.com, bbc.in

The 2026 Winter Olympics ran from February 6 to 22, marking the first time in Olympic history that two cities, Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, jointly hosted the Games. Roughly thirty five hundred athletes competed across sixteen disciplines and one hundred sixteen medal events, with skating and ice hockey held in Milan while most alpine skiing events took place in the mountain resort of Cortina. Additional snow sport venues were spread across other parts of the Italian Alps, including Bormio and Livigno, giving the Games a wider geographic footprint than most previous Winter Olympics.

The opening ceremony took place at Milan's San Siro Stadium, the shared home of two of the city's storied football clubs, with simultaneous celebrations in several other host towns and a musical lineup that included international pop stars. Ski mountaineering made its Olympic debut at these Games, and National Hockey League players competed in the Olympic tournament for the first time since 2014, adding star power to the ice hockey competition. The Games were also the first held under the International Olympic Committee's new leadership. Italy became the first country to host the Winter Olympics three separate times, following the 1956 Cortina Games and the 2006 Turin Games, and organizers closed the event with a ceremony at the Verona Olympic Arena before the Winter Paralympics began in early March.
