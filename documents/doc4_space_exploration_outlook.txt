Title: 2026 Marks a Busy Year for Space Exploration Beyond the Moon
Source (for reference, not reproduced): thedebrief.org, rocketstem.org, nasa.gov

Beyond the Artemis program, 2026 has turned into one of the busier years in recent memory for space agencies around the world. NASA opened the year with a series of spacewalks outside the International Space Station to install a new solar array, part of ongoing maintenance needed to keep the orbiting laboratory running as it ages. New crew rotations continued throughout the year, including a SpaceX Crew flight carrying astronauts for a fresh science expedition, and NASA also signed an agreement with a private company for a future commercial mission to the station, a sign of the agency's continued shift toward relying on commercial partners for routine orbital transportation.

Robotic exploration has kept pace with crewed missions. Commercial Lunar Payload Services flights are delivering scientific instruments to the lunar surface ahead of future crewed landings, laying groundwork for eventual permanent habitation on the Moon. Engineers have also been finishing construction and testing on the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, a new observatory expected to survey billions of galaxies once it reaches orbit. Further out, planetary scientists are watching for updates from a mission investigating whether Jupiter's icy moon Europa could harbor conditions suitable for life, a question that could reshape how researchers think about habitability beyond Earth. Officials have framed the combination of lunar return, space station operations, and deep space robotic missions as evidence that human space exploration is entering a new, more permanent phase rather than a series of isolated flights.
