Title: COP31 Set to Convene in Turkey Amid Shifting Global Climate Politics
Source (for reference, not reproduced): en.wikipedia.org (2026 UN Climate Change Conference), unccd.int, sustainabletechpartner.com

The 2026 United Nations Climate Change Conference, known as COP31, is scheduled to run from November 9 to 20 in Antalya, Turkey, following a competitive bidding process between Turkey and Australia to host the event. The two countries eventually settled on a shared leadership arrangement, with Turkey serving as the formal host and president of the conference while Australia takes the lead on negotiations, an approach organizers say reduces political risk and cost while still allowing both countries a central role. The conference will bring together the formal Conference of the Parties session alongside meetings tied to the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement, as well as technical subsidiary body sessions that feed into the main negotiations.

COP31 follows the agreement reached at COP30 in Brazil in 2025, which included a significant increase in adaptation finance commitments, a mechanism aimed at ensuring a fair transition away from fossil fuels for affected workers and communities, and a roadmap to halt deforestation by the end of the decade. Organizers face a more difficult political backdrop than in previous years, as the United States has scaled back several federal climate mandates and the European Union has loosened some corporate sustainability reporting requirements. Even so, companies and delegations from dozens of countries are expected to attend, and the conference is likely to remain a focal point for negotiations over emissions targets, finance, and technology transfer between wealthier and developing nations.
