Title: Bundibugyo Virus Outbreak in the DRC Becomes the Largest of Its Kind on Record
Source (for reference, not reproduced): gov.uk (Outbreaks under monitoring), who.int, hhrjournal.org

An outbreak of Ebola disease caused by the Bundibugyo virus, first declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda in mid 2026, has grown into the largest recorded outbreak of that particular strain and the second largest Ebola outbreak overall. The World Health Organization determined in May that the outbreak met the criteria for a public health emergency of international concern, a designation reserved for events considered serious enough to require a coordinated international response. By the end of August, health authorities had confirmed more than six thousand cases and over three thousand deaths in the DRC alone, spread across dozens of health zones in six provinces, with the Ituri province bearing the heaviest burden of cases and fatalities.

Uganda's portion of the outbreak was declared over in late August after health officials recorded no new cases for more than a month following the discharge of the last confirmed patient. Of the twenty confirmed cases in Uganda, most were traced back to travel from the DRC, with the remainder linked to contact with earlier patients. This marks the seventeenth documented Ebola related outbreak in the DRC since the virus family was first identified in 1976, underscoring the country's long running struggle with recurring outbreaks in remote and difficult to reach regions. WHO teams continue to monitor the situation closely alongside the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention as response efforts focus on the provinces still reporting active transmission.
