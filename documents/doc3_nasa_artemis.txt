Title: Artemis II Carries Astronauts Around the Moon for the First Time in Decades
Source (for reference, not reproduced): spaceflightnow.com, thedebrief.org, wusf.org, newscentraltv.com

In early 2026, NASA carried out the Artemis II mission, sending four astronauts on a flight around the Moon and back, the first crewed lunar journey since the Apollo program ended in 1972. The crew includes three American astronauts and one Canadian Space Agency astronaut, and their roughly ten day flight was designed to test the Orion spacecraft's life support system and communications equipment ahead of a future crewed landing planned for the Artemis III mission. The Space Launch System rocket that carried the crew rolled out to the launch pad at Kennedy Space Center in Florida in the weeks leading up to liftoff, and the mission proceeded through a flight readiness review before launch was cleared.

During the outbound trip, Orion used the Moon's gravity to bend its trajectory in a slingshot maneuver, and the crew captured images of the Earth and Moon together from deep space. The spacecraft also released small cubesats contributed by international partners including Argentina, Saudi Arabia, and South Korea. The mission concluded with Orion's splashdown in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of San Diego, watched closely by mission control and broadcast live. NASA leadership has since discussed what comes next for the Artemis program, including preparations for the crew that will attempt an actual lunar landing, a milestone that has not been achieved by any space agency since the final Apollo mission over fifty years ago.
