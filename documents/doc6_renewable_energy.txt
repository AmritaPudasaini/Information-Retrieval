Title: Solar, Wind, and Battery Storage Set to Dominate New Power Capacity in 2026
Source (for reference, not reproduced): publicinterestnetwork.org, renewablewatch.in

Government forecasts released in 2026 point to a striking shift in how new electricity generating capacity is being built. According to projections from the U.S. Energy Information Administration, solar power, wind power, and battery storage together are expected to account for the vast majority of all new generating capacity added in the country this year, with utility scale renewables and storage growing by an amount roughly equivalent to the output of dozens of large nuclear plants. If the growth materializes as projected and smaller rooftop solar installations continue at a steady pace, renewable sources are expected to approach a level that rivals natural gas in the overall American generation mix by the end of the year.

The trend is not limited to the United States. Large corporate energy buyers have continued signing long term power purchase agreements directly with renewable developers, including deals covering hundreds of megawatts of new solar and wind capacity for data centers and manufacturing operations. Investment activity has also picked up in emerging markets, with state owned and private renewable developers reporting new financing rounds, wind project awards, and green ammonia partnerships aimed at decarbonizing heavy industry. Industry analysts describe the shift as evidence that the economics of renewable power, rather than climate policy alone, are now the primary driver of investment decisions, since solar and wind projects have become the cheapest form of new generating capacity in most markets even before accounting for any environmental benefit.
